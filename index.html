About
Me

I'm a PhD researcher at the School of Computer Science, University of Birmingham, focusing on large language models and natural language generation.

My background spans several areas of machine learning. I previously worked on reinforcement learning and graph neural networks, developing agents for Google Research Football using graph convolutional networks and Deep Q-Networks. At the Chinese Academy of Sciences, I worked on EEG signal processing and brain–computer interfaces. My earlier research also involved computer vision, using lightweight convolutional neural networks for lung CT image classification.

While my current focus is on language models, my broader interests lie in understanding how machine learning systems learn, generate, and behave across different tasks.

Research

My PhD asks how language models handle long writing tasks: how different training and generation strategies change their behaviour, and what limitations emerge as texts get longer. Two studies so far:

PhD project · 2024 — now

Long-Form Text Generation of Large Language Models

01

Authorial style

A style judge, a sentence transformer fine-tuned with authorship-verification supervision and calibrated into a bounded reward, becomes the main reward for fine-tuning an 8B story generator with GRPO, an existing RL method, without the accept/reject data that DPO needs. Across Twain, Austen, Dickens and Hardy the model reaches an average style score of 0.893, above open-weight baselines.

Published · CoNLL 2026Paper →Code →

02

Reviewing creative writing

A dataset of 263,911 long-form stories, each scored by LLM judges on the 14 TTCW (Torrance Test of Creative Writing) metrics and paired with synthesised reviews. Training reviewer models on it shows that, for long and strictly structured reviews, reasoning traces made generation less stable: with them, fewer reviews were completed, and 88.6% of failed generations looped inside the trace.
